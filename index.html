Above the Cove
Late September on the Ionian coast, where the cliffs drop straight into water so deep it reads as ink. We waited an hour for a sailboat to pass through the frame. It never came, and the picture was better for it.
Meadow & Sea
A hillside of yellow wildflowers leaning into the wind, the Pacific flat and patient behind them. Shot low in the grass with the sun over one shoulder — the kind of afternoon that makes you forget to check the time.
Study in Blue
Two cuttings in a daisy-print jar, a painted wall, and nothing else. An exercise in restraint: one colour, one subject, one soft morning light. The simplest picture in this set took the longest to arrange.
Last Light
The sun drops behind the rooftops and pours sideways through the park, turning every trunk into a long blue shadow. A single walker crossed the lawn and stitched the whole composition together.
Drift
A gull riding the updraft over the harbour wall, wings set, going nowhere in particular. Stripped to black and white the sky becomes a single sheet of paper and the bird a pencil mark across it.
Front Row Seat
A yellow beanie, a snow-dusted bench, and the whole of Lower Manhattan across the river. Nobody else stopped. The city kept moving on the far shore while this one person let it be scenery for a while.
Before the Walk
Worn leather, double knots, coffee going cold on the step. The slow ritual before a long day on the trail deserves a photograph as much as the summit does — maybe more, since it's the part you can keep.
Into the Fog
The boardwalk runs twenty metres and then simply stops being visible. Sea and sky traded places all morning. Walking out to the end felt like stepping off the edge of a map, in the best possible way.
Timberline
The exact line where the forest gives up and the snow takes over, drawn across the mountain like a tide mark. Ten minutes after this frame the cloud closed in and the ridge was gone for the rest of the day.
The Resident
He was there before us and he was there when we left, sure-footed on scree that had us on all fours. Larches going gold behind him, first snow above. The mountain's actual owner, briefly tolerating visitors.
Ring Count
A century of weather written in concentric circles, read fingertip-first on a cold morning. In monochrome the grain turns to topography — ridgelines and valleys of a landscape small enough to sit on.
Red in the Snow
One figure, one red coat, and a field the wind had scoured almost white. Every winter photograph needs a single warm thing to measure the cold against — she carried it across the frame for us.
